# Slice 03 — Record payments

## Goal

Record money received against a claim's plan, allocate it to the open installments, keep an
audit trail, and expose any overpayment as credit.

## Endpoints

```text
POST /api/claims/{claimNumber}/payment-plan/payments
GET  /api/claims/{claimNumber}/payment-plan/payments
```

Recording returns `201 Created` with the payment and the updated plan. History returns payments
ordered by `paidOn`, then internal id.

## Data model

The payment ledger is append-only for this slice.

### `Payment`

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `paymentId` | Public UUID, generated by the server. |
| `amount` | Positive, scale 2. |
| `paidOn` | Required, not in the future relative to the Berlin clock. |
| `recordedAt` | Server timestamp. |
| `reference` | Optional, trimmed, maximum 255 characters, unique per plan when present. |

### `PaymentAllocation`

One row records how much of one payment went to one installment.

- `amount` is positive and scale 2.
- `(payment, installment)` is unique.
- Allocation rows are ordered by installment number in the response.

### Ledger and projection

The ledger is the audit source of truth. `installments.amount_paid` remains a persisted
projection of the allocation rows. One service is the only writer. Payments, allocations and the
installment update are written in one transaction.

This avoids a read-model rewrite while keeping an audit trail.

## Allocation algorithm

1. Load the plan and its installments.
2. Sort by due date, then installment number.
3. Walk the installments in that order.
4. Allocate `min(remainingPayment, installment.outstandingAmount())`.
5. Increase the installment's paid amount.
6. Add one allocation row for that installment.
7. Stop when the payment is exhausted or no installment remains open.

The installment paid amount never exceeds its scheduled amount.

## Overpayment and credit

- An overpayment is spread over future unpaid installments while any exist.
- Money left after every installment is paid becomes unallocated credit on the payment.
- The plan `creditAmount` is the sum of unallocated amounts across payments.
- A payment on a completed plan is accepted and recorded entirely as credit.
- A completed plan stays completed when credit is added.

Example for a plan `100 + 200`:

```text
payment 150 -> 100 to installment 1, 50 to installment 2
payment 350 -> 300 closes the plan, 50 credit
```

## Response fields

The payment response contains:

```text
paymentId, amount, paidOn, recordedAt, reference,
allocatedAmount, refundedAmount, unallocatedAmount,
allocations, refunds
```

`allocations` contains `installmentNumber`, `amount` and `dueDate`.

The updated plan contains the standard plan fields, including `creditAmount`.

## Validation and errors

| Status | Case |
| --- | --- |
| `201` | Payment recorded. |
| `400` | Missing body, null or non-positive amount, amount needing rounding, missing `paidOn`, future `paidOn`, invalid claim number, malformed JSON. |
| `404` | Unknown claim or no plan. |
| `409` | Duplicate reference, lock conflict or a data conflict. |

`reference` is optional. A blank reference becomes `null`. A duplicate non-null reference for the
same plan returns `409`.

## Concurrency

Payment recording loads the claim with a pessimistic write lock:

```java
@Lock(LockModeType.PESSIMISTIC_WRITE)
Optional<Claim> findByClaimNumberForUpdate(Long claimNumber);
```

All payments for one claim serialise. Two concurrent payments cannot both allocate against the
same outstanding amount. A lock failure maps to `409` with a retry message.

A two-thread test proves that both payments succeed, the installment is paid exactly once, and
the excess becomes credit with the expected final totals.

## Assumptions and rules

- A payment is recorded against a claim's plan, not against a single installment.
- The server allocates the payment. The client does not choose allocation rows.
- Allocation order is due date, then installment number, regardless of installment number order
  in the collection.
- Partial payments are allowed.
- One payment can span several installments.
- Overpayment is not rejected. It is spread and then kept as credit.
- `amountPaid` means money allocated to installments. It does not include credit.
- `creditAmount` is money received but not allocated after refunds.
- `paidOn` is the business payment date. `recordedAt` is when the service stored it.
- The public id is a UUID, not the internal database id.
- The ledger is append-only. Payments are not deleted or edited in this slice.
- No idempotent replay. A reference is a duplicate guard. A retry with the same reference
  returns `409`.
- Payment history is not paginated.
- No interest, fees or discounts.

## Verification

Coverage includes:

- payment factory and reference normalization;
- allocation order, partial payment, spanning and overpayment;
- payment on a completed plan;
- credit sums across payments;
- repository lookups, duplicate reference and reloaded plan credit;
- service success and all error cases;
- web recording and history endpoints;
- full plan → pay → read flow;
- two-thread concurrency.

See [Build, test and CI](00-build-test-and-ci.md).

## Out of scope

- Refunds. That is [slice 04](04-refunds-and-credit.md).
- Reversing an allocated payment or chargeback.
- Idempotent replay.
- Pagination.
- Payment deletion or editing.
